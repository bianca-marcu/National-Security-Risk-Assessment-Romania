# National Security Risk Assessment: Romania

A risk matrix and risk register covering ten major threats to Romania's security environment, from cyberattacks and the war in Ukraine to demographic decline and political polarization. Each risk is scored before and after mitigation, so the project shows both how exposed the country is and how far planned measures can reduce that exposure.

Academic project developed as part of Security Studies coursework.

## Repository contents

| File | Description |
|---|---|
| `Matrice-de-analiza-a-riscurilor_model_suport.xlsx` | Risk analysis matrix: probability, impact and exposure score for each risk, plus the classification scale |
| `Registrul_riscurilor.xls` | Risk register: causes, existing measures, new measures, residual risk and observations for each risk. Also includes the risk scale and the matrix as separate sheets |

The documents are written in Romanian.

## Methodology

**Scoring.** Each risk receives a probability score and an impact score (0-100). The exposure score is the average of the two.

| Exposure score | Level |
|---|---|
| 0-30 | Low |
| 31-60 | Medium |
| 61-100 | High |

**Classification.** Probability (low / medium / high) is crossed with impact (low / medium / high) to give a priority class:

- **A (high):** high impact with medium probability, or medium impact with high probability
- **B (medium):** medium impact with medium probability, or high impact with low probability
- **C (low):** low or medium impact with low probability

**Risk register.** For every risk the register records the factors that favor it, the *inherent risk* (before measures), the existing measures, the proposed new measures and the *residual risk* (after measures), with a short justification.

## Key findings

- **Cyberattacks are the highest inherent risk (82.5)**, driven by both high probability (80) and high impact (85). They also show the largest reduction after mitigation (to 55), reflecting the assumed effect of national cybersecurity capacity (DNSC) and NIS2 implementation.
- **Six of the ten risks start in the high band** (above 60): cyberattacks, the Ukraine conflict, civil protection capacity, political polarization, far-right radicalization and Western Balkans instability.
- **After new measures, all ten risks fall into the medium band.** The reductions come mostly from lowering probability.
- **The Ukraine conflict keeps a high impact score (75) even after mitigation.** Only its probability falls (60 to 30), because NATO collective defense and air defense reduce the likelihood of spillover but not its consequences if it occurs.
- **Climate change and population decline change least** (reductions of 8.5 and 10 points), consistent with the register's note that these are structural, slow-moving phenomena.
